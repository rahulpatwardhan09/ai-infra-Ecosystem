The two-sided view

What Upscale AI sells — and what it buys

Atomic unit sold

The fabric, on two tracks. Shipping now: open, interoperable Ethernet scale-out systems built on NVIDIA Spectrum-X switch silicon with a SONiC-based network operating system — connecting accelerators, memory and storage for distributed training and large-scale inference. In development: SkyHammer, a custom scale-up switch ASIC supporting UALink 1.0 and ESUN — up to 1,024 devices per rack at 200 Gb/s bidirectional per port — sold as a standalone ASIC for hyperscale integration or as an integrated switch blade and rack, targeting Q4 2026.

What they buy

Unlike everyone on the Compute Ladder, Upscale buys no GPUs, no power, no land — it buys the inputs to silicon: TSMC manufacturing commitments with their long lead times, NVIDIA Spectrum-X switch silicon for the shipping product line, and engineering talent (~150 people at Series A, expanding to several hundred). Its most unusual purchase is optionality itself: NVIDIA is simultaneously its silicon supplier, technology partner, and strategic investor — the incumbent it aims to disrupt is on its cap table.

The strategy

The bet

That open standards eat proprietary interconnect — and that the network, not the GPU, is where heterogeneous AI compute gets decided. Today every rack that scales up runs through NVIDIA's NVLink/NVSwitch; UALink, ESUN and Ultra Ethernet are the industry's coordinated escape hatch, and Upscale is betting it can be the first to ship a high-radix UALink switch that competes with NVSwitch head-on, selling Spectrum-X scale-out systems in the meantime to fund the wait. The hedge is elegant: if open scale-up wins, SkyHammer is the picks-and-shovels play for every non-NVIDIA accelerator; if NVIDIA holds, the Spectrum-X systems business still rides the wave — and NVIDIA's own investment suggests the incumbent wants a foot in both outcomes. The risk is sequencing: SkyHammer doesn't work alone — deployment is synced to when GPU and XPU vendors ship UALink-capable parts, a roadmap Upscale doesn't control, against competition from Broadcom, Cisco, AMD, Enfabrica and Astera Labs.

Deal-making

Natural counterparty

Hyperscalers and neoclouds building heterogeneous, bring-your-own-compute clusters — buyers writing enormous infrastructure checks who want an alternative to single-vendor lock-in. Nearly every owned-fleet company on the Compute Ladder is a prospective customer: anyone racking accelerators needs a backend fabric. On the other side of the table: XPU and GPU vendors (AMD and the custom-silicon crowd) whose UALink-capable parts SkyHammer needs to exist, the standards bodies (UALink Consortium, Ultra Ethernet Consortium, OCP for ESUN) whose specs are its product surface, TSMC for wafer allocation, and NVIDIA in its unusual triple role.

Buyer fit

Choose Upscale AI when

You're building heterogeneous or multi-vendor accelerator clusters and want open-standard fabrics — SONiC-based Ethernet scale-out on Spectrum-X today, with a committed path to UALink scale-up — and an off-ramp from proprietary interconnect lock-in as non-NVIDIA accelerators mature.

Look elsewhere when

You need proven scale-up fabric in production this quarter — NVLink/NVSwitch remains the only mature option until SkyHammer ships (Q4 2026 target) and until accelerator vendors deliver UALink-capable parts. The open scale-up ecosystem is a 2027 story; buying it in 2026 is buying a roadmap.
