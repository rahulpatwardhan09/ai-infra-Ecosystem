The two-sided view

What Vast.ai sells — and what it buys

Atomic unit sold

GPU-hours through a two-sided marketplace — buyers filter 17,000+ host-owned GPUs by spec, price, location and reliability score, from consumer RTX 4090s (~$0.34/hr in early 2026) to datacenter H100s and H200s, and run workloads in Docker containers with per-second billing. Three rental models: fixed-price on-demand, interruptible spot auctions on idle capacity, and reserved commitments. And a second product, sold to the other side: a business-in-a-box for hosts — hardware sourcing through vetted suppliers, financing qualified on platform earnings, presold enterprise demand, and a verified-datacenter certification tier.

What they buy

Nothing — the limit case of this ladder's buy side. Supply is listed, not bought: 1,400+ independent providers, from single-workstation hobbyists to data-center operators, own the hardware, set their own prices, and compete on rate. What Vast.ai builds instead of buying is trust infrastructure — reliability scoring, datacenter verification, SOC 2 Type I compliance, fraud protection, and the 24/7 support layer that hosts cite as the platform's main value.

The strategy

The bet

That liquidity beats ownership. Every owned fleet on this ladder carries capex, depreciation and utilization risk; Vast.ai's wager is that aggregating idle supply from providers who already sank the capex drives the marginal price of a GPU-hour toward electricity plus host margin — structurally below what any fleet owner can sustain. The second act is the Amazon-marketplace play: sell the sellers the operating system — hardware sourcing, financing underwritten by platform earnings history the way Amazon Lending underwrites merchants, capacity presold before it's racked — so supply compounds without Vast buying a single GPU. The known cost of the model is variance: no SLAs, no built-in autoscaling or failover, and effective prices 20–40% above the listed floor on unverified hosts once downtime is counted — which is exactly the gap the verified-datacenter tier exists to price.

Deal-making

Natural counterparty

On the demand side: cost-sensitive researchers, batch and experiment workloads, and long-tail developers who can tolerate variance or restrict themselves to verified hosts — the same population Runpod courts, minus the managed layer. On the supply side — where the marketplace's real counterparties live: GPU owners of every scale seeking demand for idle hardware, data-center operators preselling capacity before it comes online, hardware suppliers and financing partners plugged into Vast's sourcing and lending funnel. Vast.ai transacts with almost everyone on this ladder's buy side without ever holding inventory itself.

Buyer fit

Choose Vast.ai when

You're running short experiments, batch jobs or price-sensitive training where the lowest listed rates anywhere plus per-second exit beat consistency — or you can restrict to verified datacenter hosts for jobs that need predictable uptime at a still-competitive $1.50–1.87/hr for H100s.

Look elsewhere when

You need multi-day production training or SLA-bound inference — there's no built-in autoscaling, monitoring or uptime guarantee, and third-party guidance is to budget 30–50% above the lowest listed rate for predictable costs. Managed rungs of the ladder exist for exactly this.
