Before the numbers

How to read this census

A GPU count is only meaningful with its class and its date. Providers blend three kinds of numbers in their marketing, and the differences decide real capacity. Equally important is whose GPUs they are: some of the largest "clouds" below operate chips that belong to their customers.

Class 1

Deployed

Racked, networked and revenue-ready. The only number a buyer can actually consume this quarter. CoreWeave's 250,000 (Dec 2024) and Yotta's 16,384 H100s are deployed figures.

Class 2

Ordered / contracted

A signed purchase order or lease with a delivery window — real money, not yet compute. IREN's 50,000+ B300s (delivering H2 2026) and Nscale's ~200,000 GB300s for Microsoft sit here.

Class 3

Planned

An announced target with no confirmed hardware behind it. Lambda's "1M GPUs and 3 GW" ambition, Neysa's 20,000-GPU plan, TensorWave's 2 GW of secured capacity. Ambition, not capacity.

The ownership trap

Crusoe's Abilene campus runs Oracle's and OpenAI's GB200 racks; Fluidstack operates clusters owned by Anthropic (largely Google TPUs) and is building a 1 GW French site whose anchor customer is Mistral. Counting those chips as "Crusoe's fleet" or "Fluidstack's fleet" double-counts the same silicon. For landlord-operators this census records power and halls, and counts GPUs only where the provider owns the chips. Marketplace listings (Vast.ai's 1,400+ hosts, Shadeform, Akash) are other people's hardware and are never counted as a fleet.

SemiAnalysis's ClusterMAX 2.0 market view (Nov 2025) counts 209 GPU clouds, brokers and sovereign projects — 37 earned a medallion. The long tail explains why no census can stay finished.

Two calibrations for these numbers. First, the hyperscalers: AWS, Azure and Google never disclose GPU counts — though Microsoft alone admitted to 100,000+ GB300 access just through neocloud leases, and Google sells TPUs to neoclouds. Second, the single-site record isn't a cloud at all: xAI's Colossus campus in Memphis passed half a million GPUs under one roof, more than every disclosed neocloud fleet on this page combined.

Questions this page answers

Frequently asked

Is there an official list of GPU counts per cloud?

No. SemiAnalysis tracks fleet size inside its paid models and scores it under ClusterMAX's availability criterion, but publishes no per-provider table. Public figures are scattered across SEC filings, earnings calls, company newsrooms and government subsidy announcements. This page assembles them, dated and sourced.

Which neocloud has the most GPUs?

CoreWeave — last disclosed count 250,000 (Dec 2024), certainly higher now with 43 DCs, 850 MW active and $31–35B of 2026 capex. IREN states 150,000 once its B300s finish deploying in late 2026. Nscale holds the largest signed private pipeline: ~200,000 GB300 for Microsoft plus 96,000 Rubin for 2027.

Why do Crusoe and Fluidstack show no GPU count?

Much of what they build isn't their silicon: Crusoe's Abilene racks belong to Oracle/OpenAI; Fluidstack operates Anthropic's and Mistral's clusters. A single "fleet size" would double-count customer chips. The census records their power and halls instead.

What's the difference between deployed, ordered and planned?

Deployed = racked and revenue-ready. Ordered/contracted = signed purchase with a delivery window. Planned = announced target, no confirmed hardware. Every figure here carries its class and date — a 2027 plan is not a rival's 2026 capacity.

Why are ratings shown at all?

Because fleet size and fleet quality move together in buyers' minds, and ClusterMAX (v2.0, Nov 2025) is the market's de-facto quality score. But note IREN: rated "Underperforming" in Nov 2025, a 150,000-GPU Microsoft-backed fleet by Mar 2026. Ratings lag balance sheets.

A number is stale or wrong — what then?

Send it through the corrections form with a primary source. Corrections ship within a week and the stamp date moves. Private companies: a dated, verifiable fleet figure is always welcome — on the record.

Methodology

How the census is built

Primary sources only: SEC filings (S-1, 10-K, 8-K), earnings materials, company newsrooms, named-customer press releases, and government announcements (METI subsidy awards, IndiaAI Mission, Korea's national compute project). Each row shows the figure, its class (deployed / ordered / planned), the silicon, the footprint, and the source with its date. We don't count landlords' buildings as GPU fleets, don't count marketplace listings as owned hardware, and don't repeat figures we can't trace.
